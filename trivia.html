Development

In this section, I want to preserve the footprints—and the people—that led me to build this website. I started making websites when I was around ten, not in a formal or particularly skillful way, but I wrote plenty of blog-style posts. Recently, I realized it was time to start over. This reboot feels like a small reconnaissance mission—driven by nostalgia for how blogs worked and how authors connected from roughly 2000 to 2010. It was a golden era, before social media and today’s wave of AI-generated noise.

CMS

  • Quarto is recommended by Dr. Marcelo Leal at my university.
  • Blowfish was the theme I used for my previous Hugo website. It’s brilliant and easy to customize.
  • I have used WordPress for nearly two decades, including theme customization and plugin modifications.

Inspirations

  • Dr. Andrew Heiss is incredibly productive and shares many practical tips for organizing an academic website. His posts go back to 2007—it’s impressive that he has preserved so many digital footprints.
  • Dr. David Folio taught me how to create a custom publication listing with Quarto. Merci beaucoup.
  • justfont designed Hunnin, an open font based on Kosugi Maru and Varela Round. Hunnin is super friendly for Tâi-uân Lô-má-jī Phing-im Hong-àn (Taiwanese Romanization System) and is on both Google Fonts and GitHub.
